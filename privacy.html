Nook

Privacy policy

Effective October 29, 2026. Applies to the Nook extension for Chrome and other Chromium browsers.

Everything Nook saves stays in your browser, on your computer. Nook has no accounts and no servers, and it never sends your tabs, notes or thumbnails anywhere.

  • No analytics, ads or tracking.
  • Nothing is sold or shared with anyone.
  • You can delete any item, or everything, at any time.
  • If you buy Nook Lifetime, Nook checks your license key with our payment provider. That is the only thing it ever sends.

What Nook saves

When a tab rests in Nook, Nook saves what it needs to show the tab to you again and reopen it:

  • The tab’s web address, page title and site icon.
  • A small picture (thumbnail) of the page as it looked on your screen.
  • When the tab was opened, last looked at and saved.
  • The tab group it was in, and its position if you saved a whole window.
  • What you add yourself: the reason you pick, project names, notes and saved window names.
  • Your Nook settings, and simple counts such as how many tabs you tidied this month.

How thumbnails work

Nook takes a thumbnail of the tab you are looking at, about two seconds after it loads, using Chrome’s built-in screenshot feature for the visible tab. It shrinks the picture to a small image and keeps it in your browser. Nook takes thumbnails on every site by default. In Settings you can list sites that should never get one; their tabs are still saved, without a picture.

Where it is kept

Your data is stored with Chrome’s extension storage and in your browser’s local database (IndexedDB) on this computer. It is not synced to your Google account or to other devices, and Nook does not send it to the developer or to anyone else. Opening a saved tab simply loads that website, as any link would. Nook uses your saved data only to show, sort and reopen your tabs.

Notifications

If you turn on the weekly Read later reminder, Nook shows a Chrome notification built from your own saved tabs. Optional fading of unread tabs is off unless you turn it on, and it only ever deletes items after telling you first.

Nook Lifetime purchases

Nook is free. Nook Lifetime is an optional one-time purchase. Payments are handled by Dodo Payments, which acts as the merchant of record: it takes your payment details, email and billing country under its own privacy policy, published on dodopayments.com. Nook and its developer never see your card details.

  • When you activate Lifetime, Nook sends your license key and the label “Nook for Chrome” to Dodo’s license service to confirm the purchase. About once a week it checks again, so refunded keys stop working.
  • Nook keeps the key and an activation ID in your browser. Removing Lifetime from a browser in Settings frees that activation.
  • After checkout, Dodo sends you to Nook’s thank-you page with your key in the page address. The page shows the key to you and Nook reads it to turn Lifetime on. It is not sent anywhere else.
  • Dodo shares your email and purchase details with the developer so we can help with your purchase and give refunds. We use them for nothing else.

None of your tabs, notes, thumbnails or settings are part of any of this.

Your choices

  • Delete any saved tab or saved window from the Nook page.
  • Delete everything with Settings → Clear everything.
  • Export your data as JSON or Markdown from Settings.
  • Uninstalling Nook removes all of its data from your browser.

Chrome Web Store user data policy

Nook’s use of information it receives adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Nook does not use your data for advertising, does not transfer it to third parties (apart from sending your license key to the payment provider to confirm a purchase you made), and does not use it to determine creditworthiness or for lending.

Children

Nook is a general productivity tool and is not directed at children under 13.

Changes

If Nook ever starts handling data differently, for example to offer optional sync, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the effective date above will change.
