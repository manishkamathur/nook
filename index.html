How it works

Your tabs rest. You decide what they were for.

  1. Tabs rest on their own

    Tabs you haven’t looked at for 4 hours close quietly and wait in Nook, each with a picture of the page. You choose the time, from 1 hour to 1 day.

  2. Sort them in one go

    When you’re ready, give each tab a reason: Read later, Buying, To do, Research, Reference, or close it for good. Nook suggests one, so most tabs take a single key.

  3. Find them again

    Search everything, reopen one tab or a whole section as a tab group, tick off what’s done, and let the rest wait without cluttering your browser.

Questions

Good to know

Is Nook free?

Yes, with no limit on how many tabs it keeps. Nook Lifetime is an optional $15 one-time purchase that adds saved windows, the weekly reminder and fading.

How does Lifetime turn on?

After checkout, Nook turns it on by itself in the browser you bought from. Your license key is also in your receipt email: paste it in Nook’s Settings to use Lifetime on up to 3 browsers.

Can I get a refund?

Yes. Ask within 14 days for a full refund, no questions asked. Email entropy.atelier@gmail.com or reply to your receipt.

Will it close a tab I’m in the middle of?

No. Only tabs you haven’t looked at for the time you choose. The clock counts only while you’re using the browser, so a night away or a restart doesn’t sweep everything up.

What happens to a tab after it rests?

It waits in Nook with its title, site and a picture of the page. Click it to open it again. It stays in Nook until you delete it or open it with “Open and remove”.

Where are my tabs stored?

In your browser on this computer. They aren’t synced to other devices. Uninstalling Nook removes them, so export first if you want a copy.

Can I bring my tabs from OneTab?

Yes. Export your list from OneTab, then use Import in Nook’s settings. The tabs arrive in Unsorted, ready to sort.

Which browsers does it work in?

Chrome and other browsers built on Chromium, such as Edge, Brave and Arc.
